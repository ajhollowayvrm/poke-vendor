# Adjacent phenomena in the US Pokemon TCG sealed-product scene (2024 to November 2026)

Scope note: These notes exclude the topics that other notes already cover (retail allocations, flipper price cycles, case configurations, distributors, rip-and-ship, social media callouts). Each section ends with game-mechanic ideas in "Inferences". The research used about 20 tool calls. Many sources are secondary news sites. Some facts come from a single source, and the notes say so.

## Restock-alert ecosystems (cook groups, monitors, bots)

### Takeaway
Paid Pokemon restock-alert groups on Discord and Whop cost about $5 to $25 per month, and most offer a 3-day free trial. Large free servers also exist. This gives a clear "pay for information" tier above free alerts.

### Cited Findings
- Poke Alerts (Whop/Discord): $5.99 per month, or $14.99 per 3 months, with a 3-day free trial. Source is a third-party review page, not the seller. — [whopreviews: pokealerts](https://sites.google.com/view/whopreviews/pokealerts)
- PokeFinder: $6.99 per month, or $14.99 per 3 months, with a 3-day free trial. — [whopreviews: pokefinder](https://sites.google.com/view/whopreviews/pokefinder)
- PokeNotify app: $7.99 per month, billed every 30 days. — [Buttondown post](https://buttondown.com/daniel_lindqvist85/archive/pokenotify-pricing-on-whop-7-99-a-month-for-the-app/)
- PokeFreeze Notify: $7.99 per month, or $79.99 per year. — [whopreviews: pokefreeze notify](https://sites.google.com/view/whopreviews/pokefreeze-notify)
- Poke Signals: $9.99 per month on one page; another review says $19.99 per month. The sources conflict. — [glowlane452: pokesignals](https://sites.google.com/view/glowlane452/pokesignals)
- Lunch Money cook group: $25 per month for stock checkers and restock pings. — [Buttondown post](https://buttondown.com/lily_wheeler69/archive/lunch-money-pricing-on-whop-25-a-month-everything-included/); [whopreviews: lunchmoney](https://sites.google.com/view/whopreviews/lunchmoney)
- Pokecop: about 25 euros per month (one review). — [whopreviews: pokecop](https://sites.google.com/view/whopreviews/pokecop)
- One reviewer estimates that individual bot subscriptions for Pokemon restocks cost $20 to $60+ per month. This is an estimate, not a verified price. — [whopreviews: tcgtracker](https://sites.google.com/view/whopreviews/tcgtracker)
- Free alert servers exist. RestockR is a free Discord server with 46,172 members. — [RestockR Discord listing](https://discord.com/servers/pokemon-restock-alerts-restockr-1348719595619614743)
- Bots work at scale. In 2025, a scalper group claimed it bought over 42,000 Phantasmal Flames items from Pokemon Center before the site cancelled the orders. PC Gamer notes the screenshots may be fake. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/card-games/let-the-criers-cry-42-000-pokemon-tcg-phantasmal-flames-products-have-been-swiped-by-a-scalper-group-thanks-to-a-massive-error-on-the-pokemon-center); [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/the-pokemon-company-just-saved-fans-from-a-massive-42000-item-scalping-tcg-nightmare/)

### Inferences
- Game mechanic: an "alert subscription" upgrade. The free tier gives a late alert (for example, after other buyers clear the shelf). A $6 to $25 per month tier gives an earlier alert or a better restock-day forecast. A monthly cost that the player must earn back makes a clear risk-and-reward choice.
- Game mechanic: a "bot" tier is a moral and legal risk. It can give large online hauls but raises the chance of a mass order cancellation (see the Pokemon Center section).
- The price spread ($5 vs $25) suggests the game can show a quality range: cheap groups have more false alerts.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable price list for "checkout services" (paid people who check out for you). I found no first-party seller pages; all prices come from affiliate or review pages.
- I found no reliable data on bot software license prices (for example, resale prices of bot keys) for 2025 to 2026.

## Lotteries, raffles, and LGS purchase rules

### Takeaway
Walmart started an online "Collectibles Drawing" lottery in August 2026. Some local stores run their own MSRP lotteries. Some retailers make products unsellable as "sealed" to block resellers, and Target stopped this practice in August 2026 because of legal risk.

### Cited Findings
- Walmart Collectibles Drawing opened on August 24, 2026. The first Pokemon item was a Prismatic Evolutions Elite Trainer Box at $69.99, added on August 31, 2026. Entry is free and needs a Walmart account, a shipping address, a payment method, and a quantity. Walmart picks entries at random and places winning orders automatically. Walmart allows one entry per person per drawing. Resellers are not barred. The lottery covers online sales only. — [GodisaGeek, Sept 2026](https://godisageek.com/2026/09/walmart-pokemon-tcg-lottery-drawing-system/)
- Anecdote: one fan reports that a local card store ran a lottery for the Prismatic Evolutions Super-Premium Collection at MSRP. — [Dexerto](https://dexerto.com/pokemon/pokemon-retailer-takes-drastic-measures-to-combat-prismatic-evolutions-scalpers-3029143)
- A retailer limited each party to one Prismatic Evolutions product. To sell at MSRP, the retailer removed the shrink wrap and cut the corner of each booster pack. — [Dexerto](https://dexerto.com/pokemon/pokemon-retailer-takes-drastic-measures-to-combat-prismatic-evolutions-scalpers-3029143)
- Target employees sliced ETB shrink wrap in June 2026 and removed wrap at another store in August 2026. An internal Target notice (in effect August 7 to 23, 2026) told stores not to break seals "under any circumstances", to "reduce legal risk". The notice says trading card inventory is vendor-owned, not Target-owned. Target keeps enterprise-level purchase limits. — [Dexerto, Aug 2026](https://www.dexerto.com/pokemon/target-orders-stores-to-stop-cutting-open-pokemon-cards-over-legal-risk-3397840/)
- Target policy also covers "individuals that exhibit behaviors such as pack searching and weighing". — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/pokemon/target-is-now-opening-sealed-pokemon-boxes-to-combat-scalpers-3395441/)
- GameStop sells above MSRP. In November 2025, GameStop charged $7 for a sleeved single pack (MSRP $4.49) and $239.99 for a 36-pack booster box (MSRP $143.64). In early 2025, GameStop added about $10 to collection boxes. — [Kotaku, March 2026](https://kotaku.com/gamestop-pokemon-tcg-prices-booster-box-etb-perfect-order-2000681798)
- In May 2025, GameStop raised the Destined Rivals booster box to $199.99. GameStop named a stock shortage and US tariffs as reasons. — [SNKRDUNK magazine](https://snkrdunk.com/en/magazine/2025/05/29/pokemon-tcg-gamestop-raises-prices-of-destined-rivals-dri-products/)
- Feel Good Gaming, a card show in Glen Burnie, Maryland, banned vendors from selling "all sealed product that is currently for sale at retail" (April 2026). Reddit users say some other local shows have similar rules. — [ComicBook.com, April 2026](https://comicbook.com/collectibles/news/pokemon-card-show-strikes-back-at-scalpers-with-long-awaited-ban/)

### Inferences
- Game mechanic: a weekly "drawing" event. The player enters for free, and a random roll decides if the player can buy at MSRP. Multiple accounts can raise the odds but add a ban risk.
- Game mechanic: some shops sell only "opened" product at MSRP (cut wrap or cut pack corners). This product is fine to open for singles but has no sealed resale value. It forces a choice between MSRP-and-open versus marked-up sealed.
- Game mechanic: "big-box markup" stores. A chain sells at about 1.5x MSRP (GameStop: $7 per $4.49 pack, $239.99 per $143.64 box). The player can buy there when shelves are empty elsewhere, but the margin is thin.
- Game mechanic: a "no new sealed" card show. At some shows the player cannot sell current-retail sealed product. This pushes the player to bring singles, vintage, or older sealed.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced examples of the LGS rule "you must buy X to get Y" or "packs only with a purchase" for 2025 to 2026. This rule is common in community talk, but I did not find a citable report.
- I found no data on Pokemon Center in-store raffles in the US.

## Repacks, mystery boxes, and card vending machines

### Takeaway
Official TPCi vending machines grew from a maximum of 65 (2017 to 2023) to 1,473 machines in 25 states (May 2025). Third-party "Lucky Box" machines sell random graded cards, and critics call them gambling. The Pentagon removed one in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- TPCi listed 1,473 official Pokemon TCG vending machines in 25 US states on May 21, 2025. History: a maximum of 65 machines from 2017 to 2023; 120 in October 2023; fewer than 200 in six states in March 2024. TPCi added purchase limits in 2025 as an anti-scalping measure. — [PokeBeach, May 2025](https://pokebeach.com/2025/05/tpci-dramatically-increases-tcg-vending-machines-across-the-united-states)
- One report says the official machines release a set number of products at irregular intervals. A later report (date not confirmed) says some kiosks are being removed from some locations. — [Vending Times](https://www.vendingtimes.com/news/pokemon-vending-machines-may-be-on-way-out/); [SNKRDUNK magazine, March 2025](https://snkrdunk.com/en/magazine/2025/03/10/pokemon-tcg-official-pokemon-tcg-vending-machines-stricken-by-various-issues)
- Fans complain that machines are often sold out. A change.org petition asks for purchase limits on the machines. — [ComicBook.com](https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/pokemon-tcg-vending-machines-so-sad); [change.org](https://www.change.org/p/enforce-purchase-limits-on-pokémon-vending-machines/u/33291174)
- Lucky Box machines sell random Pokemon and sports cards, mostly in malls. A Lucky Box in the Pentagon food court advertised a Surging Sparks Pikachu ex worth up to $840. 404 Media calls the machines a form of gambling. — [404 Media](https://404media.co/theres-a-lootbox-with-rare-pokemon-cards-sitting-in-the-pentagon-food-court)
- The Pentagon moved the machine for an internal review, and Lucky Box removed the Pentagon from its listings. Lucky Box disputes the gambling label and says every buyer gets an item that they can resell. Coverage says most items are worth a few dollars, with a few "holy grail" items per machine. — [Casino.org](https://casino.org/news/pentagon-pulls-lucky-box-machine-after-operators-social-media-brag); [Gambling News](https://www.gamblingnews.com/news/pentagon-removes-loot-box-vending-machine-after-review/); [Vending Times](https://www.vendingtimes.com/news/regulatory-grey-area-surrounds-lucky-box-vending-machines-amid-gambling-concerns/)
- CGC promotes Lucky Box machines that hold CGC-certified cards. — [CGC Cards](https://www.cgccards.com/news/article/14140/)
- In Australia, a Guardian report raised a concern that these machines expose children to gambling-like activity. — [inkl / Guardian](https://www.inkl.com/news/fears-lucky-box-vending-machines-giving-children-a-taste-of-gambling)
- Older case (2023, Japan): a Kyoto subway card machine drew complaints because advertised odds applied to the full production lot, not to one machine's stock. — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/20230705-19948/)
- UK case: buyers at a mall machine said they got fake cards, and the operator disputed this. — [Ipswich.co.uk](https://www.ipswich.co.uk/gotta-sell-em-all-fake-cards-reported-at-new-pokemon-card-vending-machine/)

### Inferences
- Game mechanic: the player can place a "mystery vending machine" at a location (mall, food court). The player chooses the price per spin and the prize pool (mostly low-value cards, a few chase slabs). Income is steady, but a "regulator" or "news story" event can force removal. This fits the vendor role well.
- Game mechanic: official vending machines as a restock source with random timing and a per-person limit. The player can "camp" a machine, which costs time.
- Game mechanic: repack honesty. A player-made repack box with a disclosed odds table is safe. A box with a hidden bad pool gives more profit but risks a reputation hit.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced 2025 to 2026 report about a US repack or "pull box" scam lawsuit. I found no sourced data on claw machines with Pokemon cards in the US.
- Some figures on mystery-box EV (for example, tech-insider.org) have no source. I did not use them.

## Return fraud, tampering, and pack weighing

### Takeaway
Retailers act against pack weighing and searching. GameStop announced a permanent ban for pack weighing. Most reported tampering cases are theft-by-concealment or mail fraud, not ETB returns.

### Cited Findings
- GameStop announced a permanent store ban for customers who weigh packs to find rare cards. Rare or foil cards make a sealed pack slightly heavier. — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/pokemon-scalpers-tcg-gamestop-weigh-scale-lifetime-ban-2000646859)
- Target policy covers "pack searching and weighing". — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/pokemon/target-is-now-opening-sealed-pokemon-boxes-to-combat-scalpers-3395441/)
- Keith Wallis was arrested in Tallahassee, Florida, for 75 thefts at Target stores (July 2025 to February 2026). He allegedly hid cards inside taco seasoning packets, paid only for the packets, and resold the cards on eBay. Charges include felony retail theft and money laundering. — [KSL, 2026](https://www.ksl.com/article/51477273/pokmon-cards-are-igniting-an-international-crime-spree)
- UK shop Bath TCG stopped online sales after its mail orders arrived empty and resealed with tape. — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/pokemon/pokemon-card-shop-stops-online-sales-after-thieves-reseal-mail-orders-3406208/)

### Inferences
- Game mechanic: "tampered stock" risk. When the player buys loose packs or ETBs from a secondary source (a flea market, a return shelf), a small chance exists that the packs are weighed or resealed. The EV of these packs is much lower. A player who sells tampered product without knowing it takes a reputation hit.
- Game mechanic: the player can choose to sell only "fresh from case" product at a premium, because buyers trust it more.
- Game mechanic: a store ban for suspicious actions (bulk buys, weighing) at a retail source.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced 2025 to 2026 report of opened-and-returned ETBs at Target or Walmart. Community reports exist, but I found none in news.

## Theft and crime around sealed product

### Takeaway
Card theft rose in 2026. Reported events include a $963,000 FedEx truck theft near PSA (October 2026), a missing truck with pallets of 30th Celebration product (September 2026), and many smash-and-grab store robberies of $10,000 to $50,000 each.

### Cited Findings
- About $963,000 in cards was stolen from a FedEx vehicle near the PSA office in Santa Ana, California, on October 13, 2026. Thieves took two pallets (about 100 packages each) and about 50 packages from a third pallet. The cards were collector submissions for grading. PSA deactivated the cert numbers. No arrests at report time. — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/fedex-truck-robbed-of-trading-cards-worth-nearly-1-million-12593572); [Good Morning America](https://www.goodmorningamerica.com/news/story/thieves-steal-1-million-worth-pokmon-cards-socal-137407837)
- Safari Zone (Smyrna, Delaware) lost "several pallets" of 30th Celebration product on about September 16, 2026. The distributor's delivery was subcontracted to an independent driver with a private truck, and the truck was not found. Safari Zone closed to local customers for a day to reconcile preorders. It did not give the value. — [Wargamer, Sept 2026](https://www.wargamer.com/pokemon-trading-card-game/30th-celebration-van-heist)
- Tacoma, Washington: thieves drove a pickup through the rear doors of a card shop and took about $50,000 in Pokemon and Magic cards. — [Attack of the Fanboy](https://attackofthefanboy.com/news/thieves-rammed-a-pickup-through-a-tacoma-card-shops-doors-filled-the-truck-bed-with-pokemon-and-magic-cards-and-were-gone-in-minutes/)
- Graham, Washington (March 7, 2026): two thieves broke a window and filled bins with cards in under two minutes. Loss about $10,000. — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51477273/pokmon-cards-are-igniting-an-international-crime-spree)
- Abbotsford, BC (March 2026): about $25,000 in cards plus about $10,000 in damage. — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51477273/pokmon-cards-are-igniting-an-international-crime-spree)
- Shops in Las Vegas, New York City, the Vancouver area, and Nottingham were hit in 2026, with combined losses over $500,000. — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51477273/pokmon-cards-are-igniting-an-international-crime-spree)
- Anecdote: content creator PokeDean said thieves emptied his home and took only his most expensive cards (video posted February 2026). — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51477273/pokmon-cards-are-igniting-an-international-crime-spree)
- Why cards are targets: Nick Jarman (Certified Trading Card Association) says cards are compact, liquid, and "nearly impossible to trace" because they have no serial numbers. A shop owner says insurers are less willing to cover card shops. — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51477273/pokmon-cards-are-igniting-an-international-crime-spree)

### Inferences
- Game mechanic: inventory theft risk that grows with the value stored at the shop or home. The player can buy a safe, cameras, or insurance. Insurance premiums rise as the hobby heats up, and an insurer can refuse coverage.
- Game mechanic: a "missing shipment" event. A distributor order does not arrive, and the player must refund or delay preorder-like commitments (keep this to customer holds, because the game rejects preorders).
- Game mechanic: graded cards have cert numbers and are traceable; sealed and raw cards are not. A stolen-goods buy offer at a low price is a moral choice with a police risk.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced card-show theft incident for 2025 to 2026 with a dollar value.

## Pokemon Center online: queue, exclusives, and cancellations

### Takeaway
The Pokemon Center international site added a virtual queue in early 2025. Since then, it has cancelled large waves of orders after hot launches (Destined Rivals 2025, Phantasmal Flames 2025, Ascended Heroes 2026, 30th Celebration September 2026). Real fans also lose orders in the purges.

### Cited Findings
- The Pokemon Center international site added a queue in early 2025. The Japanese site used a queue for years before that. — [SNKRDUNK magazine, Feb 2025](https://snkrdunk.com/en/magazine/2025/02/06/pokemon-tcg-pokemon-center-international-website-implements-queue-system)
- March 2025: buyers waited hours in the queue for Destined Rivals ETBs and Booster Bundles. Later, the site cancelled some completed orders without an apology. — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2025/03/select-pokemon-card-pre-orders-cancelled-after-new-expansions-disastrous-launch)
- September 2026: Pokemon Center cancelled thousands of 30th Celebration orders. Collectors think bot orders were the target. Some manual buyers who stayed within limits also lost orders. TPC gave no statement. — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/pokemon-tcg-30th-celebration-orders-cancelled-bots-scalpers-center-company-2000732116); [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/pokemon/pokemon-tcg-fans-lose-their-orders-in-pokemon-center-crackdown-on-scalpers-bots-3406602/)
- Ascended Heroes orders (early 2026) were also cancelled. — [Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/pokemon-trading-card-game/ascended-heroes-preorders-cancelled)
- Plush orders were cancelled as "oversold", and refunds came without warning. — [OpenCritic news](https://opencritic.com/news/12998/pok-mon-plushies-are-not-safe-from-scalpers-as-the-pok-mon-center-issues-refunds-without-warning)
- Pokemon Center started an "Early Access" system for popular items. I could not load the article text, so the rules are unknown. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/tabletop-gaming/pokemon-center-tackles-resellers-with-early-access-for-cards-merch-and-more-and-its-about-bloody-time/)

### Inferences
- Game mechanic: an "online drop" mini-event. The player joins a queue with a random position. A win gives MSRP product (for example, a Pokemon Center ETB). Days later, a cancellation roll can remove the order. The roll is higher for bulk or multi-account orders, but it is never zero.
- Game mechanic: the Pokemon Center ETB is a separate SKU with a higher resale value than the retail ETB.

### Gaps

## Promo-card events and reseller swarms

### Takeaway
The US McDonald's "Dragon Discovery" promo (January 21 to February 24, 2025) drew resellers, who listed sealed cases on eBay. The Japan promo in August 2025 ended early after resellers threw away meals.

### Cited Findings
- US McDonald's Dragon Discovery promo ran January 21 to February 24, 2025. Each Happy Meal had a 4-card pack. Resellers listed sealed cases on eBay. — [Escapist](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/all-mcdonalds-pokemon-tcg-promo-cards-2025-full-card-list-and-prices/); [Dexerto](https://dexerto.com/pokemon/pokemon-tcg-mcdonalds-cards-are-already-being-resold-for-absurd-prices-3038444)
- Limits in the US were not consistent. Some locations capped purchases at four Happy Meals per person. — [Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/?p=357938)
- Japan (August 2025, out of scope but central to the phenomenon): McDonald's Japan ended the promo early after resellers bought meals for the cards and threw away the food. Cards sold for about $28 each on eBay, against a 510 yen meal. McDonald's Japan set a five-meal limit and agreed to share data with Mercari. — [AOL / USA Today](https://www.aol.com/mcdonalds-japan-ends-pok-mon-173948184.html); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/McDonald-s-Pokemon-TCG-promotion-in-Japan-ends-in-chaos-with-scalpers-leaving-wasted-food.1084160.0.html)

### Inferences
- Game mechanic: a limited "fast-food promo" week. The player can buy promo packs cheaply with a per-visit cap. The packs have a short price spike and then fade. A "promo ended early" event can make remaining stock more valuable.

## Tariffs, MSRP, and print supply (2025 to 2026)

### Takeaway
The US single-pack MSRP rose from $3.99 to $4.49 (date not confirmed). Tariffs in 2025 raised accessory and some product costs, and retailers used tariffs as a reason for markups. TPC says it prints "at maximum capacity", and total print volume grows by about 10 billion cards per year.

### Cited Findings
- US single-pack MSRP is $4.49, up from $3.99. A 36-pack box MSRP is $143.64, which matches the old $3.99 pack price. ETBs usually cost $50 to $60 by set. — [Kotaku, March 2026](https://kotaku.com/gamestop-pokemon-tcg-prices-booster-box-etb-perfect-order-2000681798)
- April 2025: retailers in North America saw sharp price increases after tariffs expanded. A Canadian store owner reported increases of over 20% on nearly all products. Some ETB components are made in China. Sleeves and deck boxes were also hit. — [Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/?p=708067); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/board-games/what-the-skyrocketing-china-us-tariffs-mean-for-you-the-board-game-fans)
- TPC statements in January 2025 (Prismatic Evolutions) and May 2026 used the words "maximum capacity" and said TPC is "working to print more". The May 2026 link to Mega Evolution Chaos Rising (May 22, 2026) comes from one blog only. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/tabletop-gaming/the-pokemon-company-addresses-prismatic-evolutions-and-tcg-shortages-as-scalpers-feast-we-are-actively-working-to-print-more/); [The Card Shop Finder](https://thecardshopfinder.com/blog/pokemon-company-tcg-shortage-reprints-destined-rivals-prismatic-evolutions-20260527/)
- Print figures: ComicBook.com lists 85 billion+ (2026), 75 billion+ (2025), 64.8 billion+ (2024), 52.9 billion+ (2023). PokeBeach says 64.8 billion is the total ever produced, so these are cumulative totals, not yearly prints. That means about 10 billion new cards per year. ComicBook.com presents them as if they were yearly. TPC is building a new campus that will not be complete before 2027. — [ComicBook.com, May 2026](https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/new-report-confirms-pokemon-tcg-massively-increased-production-in-2026-but-its-still-not-enough/); [PokeBeach](https://pokebeach.com/2025/05/tpci-dramatically-increases-tcg-vending-machines-across-the-united-states)
- TPCi printed 30.7 billion of the 64.8 billion cards ever produced in the three fiscal years before May 2025 (47.4%). — [PokeBeach](https://pokebeach.com/2025/05/tpci-dramatically-increases-tcg-vending-machines-across-the-united-states)
- The current shortage started with Surging Sparks (November 2024). — [GodisaGeek](https://godisageek.com/2026/09/walmart-pokemon-tcg-lottery-drawing-system/)
- For Black Bolt and White Flare (2025), most US stores that asked for stock got no allocation. Distributors set their own prices; only TPCi MSRP is fixed. — [PokeBeach](https://pokebeach.com/2025/05/tpci-dramatically-increases-tcg-vending-machines-across-the-united-states)

### Inferences
- Game mechanic: an "MSRP step" event on an era change (for example, $3.99 to $4.49 per pack). Old stock bought at the old cost gets a margin boost.
- Game mechanic: a "tariff" event raises supply costs (sleeves, toploaders, and some boxed product) by up to 20% for a period. Retailers then mark up, which also raises the player's resale ceiling.
- Game mechanic: "maximum capacity" statements as a weak signal. A TPC reprint statement can lower sealed prices over the next weeks.
