# Pokemon TCG (English) sealed cases: configurations, prices, and economics (November 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-20. English product only.

Source notes:
- "PHD" means the PHD Games distributor solicitation pages. PHD is a US hobby distributor. Each page gives the item code, the case count, and the MSRP. The MSRP is for the full case when the line says "Case".
- "TCGplayer mkt" means the TCGplayer market price from the tcgcsv.com daily archive. The archive timestamp is `2026-11-20T20:06:21+0000`. The market price is a recent-sales estimate. It is not a guaranteed sale price.
- Product IDs (`id=`) are TCGplayer product IDs. URL pattern: `https://www.tcgplayer.com/product/<id>`.
- Group files: `https://tcgcsv.com/tcgplayer/3/<groupId>/products` and `.../prices`.

## 1. Units per case for each product type

### Takeaway
The current (2026) Mega Evolution era uses fixed case counts. A booster display case holds 6 booster boxes (216 packs). An ETB case holds 10 ETBs. A booster bundle case holds 25 bundles. The other counts are: sleeved booster case 144 packs, 3-pack blister case 24 blisters, Build & Battle display 10 kits (6 displays per case), UPC case 4, and most collection boxes and tins 6 per case. Special sets have no booster box. For those sets the case products are the ETB case, the booster bundle case or display, the mini tin display, and the collection cases.

### Inferences
- MSRP per pack in 2026 is $4.49. A booster display at $161.64 is exactly 36 x $4.49. A sleeved booster case at $646.56 is exactly 144 x $4.49. The game can use $4.49 as the base pack MSRP for 2026.
- Older guides use a box MSRP of about $144 (36 x $3.99). That price is pre-2025. The 2026 MSRP is $161.64.
- The 30th Celebration UPC per-unit MSRP computes to $177.21. This is an odd number. PHD may round, or the UPC MSRP may be $179.99 with a case discount. Treat $179.99 as the retail tag and $708.84 as the case MSRP.
- For a game, the safe case counts are: booster box 6, ETB 10, Pokemon Center ETB 4 (one source only), booster bundle 25, sleeved booster 144 packs, 3-pack blister 24, B&B display 10 (6 displays per case), mini tin display 10 tins (4 displays per case), UPC 4, collection boxes 6, tins 6, Knock Out / 2-pack collections 24, Tech Sticker collections 12.

### Gaps
- No primary TPCi source gives a Pokemon Center ETB case count. Only one seller listing says 4.
- PHD does not show a unit count for the Poster Collection Case. The count of 6 is computed from the price.
- No source gave an official UPC case count for the 151 UPC. The 30th Celebration UPC case is 4.
- Older-set booster bundle cases (for example Prismatic Evolutions, Surging Sparks "LGS" case) may not be 25. I did not confirm those counts.
- Big-box retail packs (Target, Walmart, Costco exclusives) ship in retailer-specific cartons. I found no counts for them.

## 2. Wholesale price vs MSRP vs market price per case (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
MSRP per case is public on distributor pages. Distributor (wholesale) prices are not public. The only published figures say that authorized hobby distributors sell at about 40-55% below MSRP. That gives about $80-$90 per booster box (against the old $144 MSRP) and about $35 per ETB. In November 2026, the TCGplayer market price for a current-set case is about 1.6-2.2 times the case MSRP. Hot sets reach 3-4 times MSRP.

### Cited Findings

Wholesale:
- A vending-industry guide says GTS Distribution, Southern Hobby Supply, Alliance Game Distributors, and ACD Distribution "offer roughly 50% off MSRP". — [Digital Media Vending wholesale guide](https://www.digitalmediavending.com/pokemon-card-wholesale-sourcing-distributor-guide)
- The same guide says: "authenticated product at 40–55% below MSRP, depending on volume and product category". Its table gives booster pack wholesale about $2.20-$2.50 against $4.49 MSRP, booster box about $85 against about $144 MSRP, ETB about $35 against $49.99, Build & Battle about $9 against $14.99, and tin about $15 against $24.99. — [Digital Media Vending wholesale guide](https://www.digitalmediavending.com/pokemon-card-wholesale-sourcing-distributor-guide)
- The same guide says distributors often need a first order of $250-$500 and have 2-4 volume tiers at about $500, $1,000, and $5,000+ per month. It says hot-release windows bring strict quantity caps (allocation). — [Digital Media Vending wholesale guide](https://www.digitalmediavending.com/pokemon-card-wholesale-sourcing-distributor-guide)
- An older guide (about 2022) said a box cost $80-$85 from a distributor and retailed near $120. It says only approved accounts get wholesale prices and that margins are thin. — [Indoor Game Bunker](https://indoorgamebunker.com/how-to-become-a-pokemon-distributor/) (old; prices are from about 2022)

### Inferences
- A store's case cost is about 50-60% of case MSRP. For a 2026 booster box case: MSRP $969.84, store cost about $485-$580 (about $81-$97 per box). For an ETB case: MSRP $499.90, store cost about $250-$300 ($25-$30 per ETB, or the $35 figure above). This is an estimate from one vending guide (which sells to vending operators and has a reason to show good margins) and one old guide. Mark it as an estimate in the game.
- The $85 box wholesale in the vending guide matches the old $144 MSRP (about 59% of MSRP). At the 2026 $161.64 MSRP, the same ratio gives about $95 per box.
- A new set at release trades near or a little above MSRP for booster box cases (ratio 1.03-1.12 for Perfect Order through Delta Reign). ETB cases and bundle cases trade at 1.35-1.5 times MSRP at release, because big-box stores sell ETBs and bundles near MSRP but in small amounts.
- A set in high demand (Destined Rivals, Phantasmal Flames, Black Bolt, Ascended Heroes, Prismatic) reaches 2-3.6 times case MSRP within 6-18 months. Sets more than 3 years old reach 7-15 times MSRP.

### Gaps
- I found no public price sheet from GTS, Southern Hobby, PHD, Alliance, or ACD with dollar wholesale prices. Those prices are behind dealer logins.
- I found no LGS-owner forum post with a verified 2025-2026 cost per box. Reddit search did not return such posts.
- The MSRP of the 2025 booster displays (Phantasmal Flames, Mega Evolution, Destined Rivals) is assumed to be the same as 2026 ($161.64). I did not confirm the date of the change from $3.99 to $4.49 per pack.

## 3. "Case fresh" and the sealed case premium

### Takeaway
A sealed case does not always sell above the sum of its units. In the TCGplayer data on 2026-11-20, booster box cases trade at 0.93-1.18 times six single boxes (median about 1.0). ETB cases trade at 1.03-1.30 times ten single ETBs (median about 1.13). Bundle cases range 0.84-1.26. The premium grows as a set ages and when the set is in demand. "Case fresh" is a seller claim that no one searched the boxes. It is a trust signal, not a proven increase in pull value.

- A guide says "Buying a full case typically saves 8–12% per box versus singles". - Sellers on TCGplayer list case SKUs separately from unit SKUs, so a case has its own market price. — [tcgcsv Delta Reign products](https://tcgcsv.com/tcgplayer/3/24831/products)

### Inferences
- New-release booster box cases trade a little below 6 single boxes (about 0.93-1.0). The reason is likely that case buyers are bulk buyers who want a lower price per box, and single-box sellers charge for the split and the shipping.
- ETB cases carry a steady premium of about 5-30%. Likely reasons: a sealed ETB case proves the ETBs are not searched; collectors keep ETB cases as a sealed asset; and big-box stores sell single ETBs, so single ETBs are easier to find than cases.
- The premium is larger for sets with high demand or older sets (Twilight Masquerade 1.18 box case; White Flare 1.30 ETB case). Mini tin cases for some old sets (151, Crown Zenith) trade below the sum of their displays. The mini tin data is noisy because the per-case count (32 or 40 tins) differs by set.
- Game model suggestion: case price = units x unit price x factor. Use factor 0.95 for a booster box case in the first year, rising to about 1.1-1.2 after 2 years. Use factor 1.05 for an ETB case at release, rising to about 1.15-1.3. Use factor 0.95-1.1 for a bundle case.
- "Case fresh" boxes for grading: a sealed case protects the boxes from shelf wear, so case-fresh boxes are the best input for a box grading service or for a premium sealed sale. I found no price data for this effect.
- "Case break" content: streamers open full cases on stream and sell spots. A case break needs a sealed case so viewers trust the result. (The group break was rejected for the game; see project memory.)

### Gaps
- I found no study that measures whether case-fresh boxes pull better than shelf boxes.
- I found no sales data for graded sealed boxes against ungraded boxes.
- The single-unit ETB price for Mega Evolution (two variants) and Journey Together (no case mkt) was not usable, so those sets have no ETB ratio.

## 4. Case seals, master case labels, UPC/SKU, reseal detection, and case scams

### Takeaway
Each case has its own distributor item code (for example `PKU10438` for the Delta Reign ETB Case). TCGplayer and retailers list the case as its own SKU. I found no public description of the TPCi master case label format. Reseal detection relies on tape, glue, shrink seams, weight, and the seller. No single check is proof. The best-known case scam is the 2022 Logan Paul case of "1st Edition Base Set" boxes. The boxes were resealed and held G.I. Joe packs.

### Cited Findings
- Distributor item codes per case: Delta Reign ETB Case `PKU10438`, Booster Bundle Case `PKU10439`, Booster Display `PKU10446`; 30th ETB Case `PKU10447`, UPC Case `PKU10448`; Flowing Fists Sleeved Booster Case `PKU10743`. — [PHD Delta Reign](https://www.phdgames.com/2026/08/20/pokemon-tcg-mega-evolution-delta-reign/), [PHD 30th](https://www.phdgames.com/2026/07/10/pokemon-tcg-30th-celebration/), [PHD Flowing Fists](https://www.phdgames.com/2026/11/19/pokemon-tcg-mega-evolution-flowing-fists/)
- Logan Paul case: the case cost $3.5 million. Its boxes were resealed with G.I. Joe card packs inside. BBCE's Matt Allen first bought the case for $2.7 million from The Meelypops Shop. Third-party authentication did not stop the fraud. — [CGM](https://www.cgmagonline.com/news/logan-paul-fake-pokemon-cards-2022)
- In Japan, a reseller found 100 special boxes that someone opened and resealed, with the exclusive promo cards removed. — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/re-shrink-wrapped-pokemon-tcg-boxes-are-now-a-thing-in-japan/) (Japanese product; shows the method)
- Forum users say you cannot fully verify a sealed case from the outside. One user says you find out only when you open the case and look at the glue. Another says some sources put identifiers on boxes and cases to trace resealed product. — [Elite Fourum thread](https://www.elitefourum.com/t/help-on-sealed-japanese-booster-box-booster-case-legitimacy/52238)
- Reseal signs for boxes and ETBs: tape residue under the shrink wrap; a second overlapping seam (factory wrap has one continuous seam per panel); flaps that do not sit flush or a visible gap; soft "pillowy" corners from re-shrink; regluing at the edges. Weight is a supporting clue only, because modern packs hold a code card and standard contents. — [Pushas](https://pushas.com/en-us/blogs/editorial/how-to-tell-if-a-pokemon-booster-box-is-resealed), [Delightful TCG](https://www.delightfultcg.com/blogs/articles/how-to-tell-if-a-pokemon-booster-box-has-been-resealed), [TCG Smiths](https://tcgsmiths.com/pokemon-reseal-scams/)
- Trust ranking for sellers: Pokemon Center direct at the top; unverified sellers at the bottom. — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/pokemon-mystery-box/)

### Inferences
- A factory case is a brown corrugated carton with factory tape and a printed or stuck label. The label carries the product name, item code, count, and a case barcode. This is the normal pattern for distributor cases. I did not find a TPCi-specific photo or description to confirm the label fields.
- Game mechanics that match the facts: a resealed case can be a scam event. The player can check tape, label, and weight before purchase. Each check lowers the risk but does not remove it. A trusted seller (a distributor) lowers the risk more than any check.

### Gaps
- I found no source for the TPCi master case label layout, case barcode format, or case UPC numbers.
- I found no documented English modern-era case reseal scam with names and dates other than the Logan Paul (vintage) case.

## 5. Box mapping and case distribution: myths and facts

### Takeaway
No reliable public data shows guaranteed hit counts per case for English Pokemon. One secondhand source says Pokemon mapping happened at least once and was fixed. Claims such as "one Charizard per Shining Fates ETB case" or "12-14 illustration rares per Temporal Forces case" have no sourced data. One Piece TCG had documented fixed per-box hits, so mapping talk from that game spreads to Pokemon.

### Cited Findings
- Mapping (the factory places packs so high-value packs are easy to find) "has been demonstrated at least once in Pokemon products". The issue was addressed, but "many aren't convinced" that mapping is gone. — [Grail Bible](https://grailbible.com/the-truth-about-rare-cards/)
- The claim that each Shining Fates ETB case had a guaranteed Charizard is "mostly armchair speculation". — [Grail Bible](https://grailbible.com/the-truth-about-rare-cards/)
- A guide claims a Temporal Forces case "typically contains 12–14 illustration rares distributed unevenly across six boxes". It cites no data. It also claims that scalpers weigh boxes, scan barcodes, or track print-run sequences to pick the best box. — [PokeCardFinder](https://pokecardfinder.com/how-many-booster-boxes-in-a-case/) (low-quality SEO source)
- One Piece TCG older sets had a fixed hit count and position per box. OP17 removed fixed mapping, so hits now land 1-4 per box at random. The data is from 1,000+ packs. — [TCGTalk](https://tcgtalk.com/guides/op17-case-mapping-calculator) (different game)

### Inferences
- For the game, model each pack as an independent draw from the set's pull rates. Do not give guaranteed hits per case. This matches the evidence.
- An optional, documented flavor: a rare "loaded box" rumor. The game can show the rumor but must not make it true, because no data supports it.

### Gaps
- I found no large-sample case-opening dataset for English Pokemon that tests per-case or per-box hit distribution.

## 6. Storage and handling: space, weight, damage risk, insurance

### Takeaway
I found almost no sourced numbers for English Pokemon case weight or dimensions. Sources say the main damage risks are crushed corners, dents, and torn shrink, and that a sealed case protects units from those risks. Insurance data was not found.

### Cited Findings
- A guide says the six-box format fits standard pallets and backroom shelves, and that it lets distributors predict shipping weight. It gives no weights. — [PokeCardFinder](https://pokecardfinder.com/how-many-booster-boxes-in-a-case/) (low-quality SEO source)
- A guide on sealed storage in the USA covers buying, checking, and storing sealed product. — [PokeTherapy guide](https://poketherapy.com/blogs/news/ultimate-guide-to-pokemon-sealed-pokemon-products-usa-buy-authenticate-store-safely)
- Third-party acrylic cases exist for a 10-bundle booster bundle display, which shows that collectors protect displays from damage. — [PokeCompare](https://pokecompare.com/blog/pokemon-booster-bundle-display), [eBay acrylic case for 10x bundle display](https://www.ebay.com/itm/316019817414)
- A guide says to buy a case only if you have storage space and plan to hold or flip within 12-18 months. — [PokeCardFinder](https://pokecardfinder.com/how-many-booster-boxes-in-a-case/) (low-quality SEO source)

### Inferences
- Rough physical sizes from the contents (estimate, not sourced): a booster box case holds 6 boxes; an ETB case holds 10 ETBs and is the largest common case; a bundle case holds 25 small boxes. For the game, use "space units": booster box case = 6 box-spaces, ETB case = about 10-15 box-spaces.
- Damage: a sealed case keeps units "case fresh". An opened case exposes each unit to shelf wear, which lowers its sealed value. This fits the market data, where ETB cases carry a premium.

### Gaps

- No source gave insurance cost or practice for sealed inventory.

## 7. How LGSs and vendors use cases

### Takeaway
Stores buy by the case from authorized distributors at about 40-55% below MSRP, subject to allocation caps on hot sets. Stores then break cases: they sell boxes, sell single packs, and open some product for singles. A full case at market sells at about the same price as its units for booster boxes, so the store's profit comes from the wholesale discount and from selling units or packs, not from a case premium.

### Cited Findings
- Distributors require a reseller account; only approved accounts get wholesale prices. — [Indoor Game Bunker](https://indoorgamebunker.com/how-to-become-a-pokemon-distributor/) (about 2022)
- Distributors use volume tiers and cap quantities during hot releases. — [Digital Media Vending](https://www.digitalmediavending.com/pokemon-card-wholesale-sourcing-distributor-guide)
- Retailers sell the same set as single units, full cases, and broken units: for example a Canadian store lists "Elite Trainer Box/Case" and a "Mini Tins Display/Case" as one product with both options. — [Pastime Sports ETB/Case](https://pastimesports.ca/products/pokemon-scarlet-violet-elite-trainer-box), [Pastime Sports 30th mini tin display/case](https://pastimesports.ca/products/pokemon-30th-celebration-mini-tins-display-case)
- TCGplayer lists "Half Booster Box" SKUs (18 packs). A guide says half boxes are not a factory product; sellers create them by splitting boxes. — [tcgcsv Destined Rivals](https://tcgcsv.com/tcgplayer/3/24269/products), [PokeCardFinder](https://pokecardfinder.com/how-many-booster-boxes-in-a-case/)
- A guide says leftover boxes from opened cases can carry "cherry-pick risk". — [PokeCardFinder](https://pokecardfinder.com/how-many-booster-boxes-in-a-case/) (low-quality SEO source)
- One commentary says big retailers add a price lift on booster boxes and ETBs during launch windows. — [TechYorker](https://techyorker.com/?p=466063)

### Inferences
- Store economics per 2026 booster box case (estimate): cost about $485-$580. Sold as 6 boxes at MSRP: $969.84. Sold as 216 loose packs at $4.49: $969.84 (same MSRP). Sold at Delta Reign market: about $1,087-$1,165. Single packs at a store often sell above $4.49 when the set is hot, so loose-pack sales can beat box sales.
- For the game, the vendor choices are: (1) sell the sealed case; (2) sell units at unit market price; (3) sell loose packs; (4) open for singles. The best choice depends on the factor table in section 3 and on the set's pack expected value.

### Gaps
- I found no first-person 2025-2026 LGS owner data (Reddit or forum) on margins, allocation size, or the split between opened and sealed sales.
