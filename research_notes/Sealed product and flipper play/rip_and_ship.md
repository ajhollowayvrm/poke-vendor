# Pokemon TCG Rip and Ship on Live Platforms (US, as of November 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-20. Notes are in Simplified Technical English. Facts older than 2026 show their year. Allegations are marked "alleged" or "allegation".

## Definition: rip and ship, and how it differs from other live formats

### Takeaway
Rip and ship is one buyer who buys one whole sealed product (a pack, a box, or a case). The seller opens it live on camera for that buyer. All contents belong to that one buyer. A group break (card break) is different: the contents of the sealed product go to MULTIPLE buyers. Whatnot policy uses exactly this line. Whatnot calls rip and ship "personals" and exempts it from most of its breaks policy.

### Cited Findings
- Whatnot defines a card break as follows: "A Card Break is a sale format where the contents of sealed product(s) are split among multiple buyers." The buyers buy cards by set criteria, and nobody knows the specific cards until the opening. — [Whatnot Card Breaks Policy (page dated 2026-06-22)](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107485220237-Card-Breaks-Policy)
- Whatnot policy: the sale of an entire sealed product, opened live for ONE person, is "not otherwise subject to the remainder of this policy". The page calls this format "personals" or "rip-and-ship". The visibility rules still apply to it. — [Whatnot Card Breaks Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107485220237-Card-Breaks-Policy)
- Whatnot visibility rule: the full display must stay on screen from the sale until the end of the opening. Multiple cameras are permitted. If the seller removes sealed or opened product from view before the reveal, that is a violation. — [Whatnot Card Breaks Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107485220237-Card-Breaks-Policy)
- TikTok Shop (US gambling policy, published 2026-06-25) still permits a "Personal Break": a buyer gets a complete sealed pack, box, or case, and the seller opens it live for that buyer. The source gives no verbatim definition. — [PPC Land on TikTok Shop gambling policy](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shop-kills-8-break-formats-and-bans-lucky-spins-under-gambling-rules/)
- TikTok Shop policy defines a break in general as "a sealed pack, box, or case of items is opened typically during a livestream." — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shop-kills-8-break-formats-and-bans-lucky-spins-under-gambling-rules/)
- Group-break subtypes named in platform rules: Random Team Breaks, Draft-Style Breaks, Bounty Breaks, Pull Games Breaks, Points Program Breaks, Train Games, King of the Hill, HP Battles (all banned on TikTok Shop US since 2026-06-25). — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shop-kills-8-break-formats-and-bans-lucky-spins-under-gambling-rules/)
- "HP Battle" is a Pokemon-specific format. Each entrant opens a pack, and only the card with the highest (or lowest) HP value wins. TikTok's gambling page says this format is common in Pokemon streams, and TikTok bans it. — [TikTok Shop Gambling Policy (search summary)](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=2903157654996737&lang=en)
- "Hit draft" breaks: a buyer is guaranteed a card from the break, so the seller may sell as many spots as there are cards. In a team break, a buyer can get no card from the break, so "every person must be shipped, at a minimum, one card for each purchase." — [Whatnot Card Breaks Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107485220237-Card-Breaks-Policy)
- Case break: TikTok Shop lists "Case Breaks" as a separate permitted format for qualified sellers. Sources disagree on whether case breaks are banned or permitted. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shop-kills-8-break-formats-and-bans-lucky-spins-under-gambling-rules/); conflict noted in [TikTok Shop search summary](https://www.scommerce.com/tiktok-shop-gambling-rules/)
- Repacks / mystery packs: California Penal Code 319.3 defines a sports trading card "grab bag" as a sealed package of cards removed from the manufacturer's original packaging. This definition covers repacks. It does not appear to cover a manufacturer-sealed pack. — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/insights/102o3q3/the-future-of-card-breaking-and-repacks-insights-from-california-litigation)
- Whatnot "Professionally Sealed Surprise Products" policy took effect 2025-11-03. Repack sellers must be pre-approved and the product must be on an "Identified Product List". Each manufacturer must publish a checklist for each series. Sellers must not have non-public knowledge of contents. — [SI](https://www.si.com/collectibles/whatnot-creating-organizational-requirements-for-repackers-on-its-platform); [List Perfectly (2025)](https://listperfectly.com/?p=11101); [Cllct (2025)](https://www.cllct.com/sports-collectibles/sports-cards/whatnot-to-regulate-sale-of-repacks-surprise-products)
- Pokemon is on Whatnot's trading-card identified-product list, and PSA-certified repacks are included. From 2026-12-07, graded items with NFC or RFID chips in repacks must be shielded. — [Underpriced Whatnot guide 2026](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/selling-pokemon-cards-on-whatnot-guide-2026)
- Wheel spins: Whatnot bans physical wheels and digital wheels (third-party sites or streaming software) and physical machines (vending, gumball, pinball, raffle drums) in breaks. — [Whatnot Card Breaks Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107485220237-Card-Breaks-Policy)
- TikTok Shop US bans lucky spins, raffles, sweepstakes, wheels, and draws. Giveaways must use the TikTok LIVE Giveaway feature, not a spin or a wheel. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shop-kills-8-break-formats-and-bans-lucky-spins-under-gambling-rules/)
- Pull boxes: Whatnot's Surprise Products policy (effective 2025-03-10) permits a pull-box game only when every pack is visible before purchase and all chase prizes are disclosed in advance. Stickers that let a buyer pick a prize from a second box are likely prohibited. This is a seller's interpretation, not official text. — [PokeMaster Mates (2025-02-10)](https://pokemastermates.com/blogs/news/whatnotsuprisebox-policyupdates)
- "Rip Until You Hit" (RTYH): the number of packs depends on what the buyer pulls. A seller blog says RTYH "may potentially violate" the Whatnot surprise policy, unless the seller states a fixed range (example: at least 6 and at most 12 packs). TikTok Shop permits "Pull-Til-You-Win Breaks" for qualified sellers. — [PokeMaster Mates (2025)](https://pokemastermates.com/blogs/news/whatnotsuprisebox-policyupdates); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shop-kills-8-break-formats-and-bans-lucky-spins-under-gambling-rules/)
- Whatnot Surprise Sets: the set must be the show's main format, every product must be revealed on air, the seller must post a full checklist, and the seller must not state a floor, ceiling, or average value. — [Underpriced Whatnot guide 2026](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/selling-pokemon-cards-on-whatnot-guide-2026)
- eBay Live: rip-and-ship listings exist with titles such as "(Live) 1x Pokemon Booster Pack As Seen On Screen (Rip&Ship)". — [eBay listing](https://www.ebay.de/itm/277409034223)

### Inferences
- The exact test that separates rip and ship from a group break: how many buyers own the contents of ONE sealed unit. One buyer = rip and ship ("personal"). Two or more buyers = a break. The game can use this test as its rule.
- A "pack auction" (a sealed pack sold by live auction, then opened on stream for the winner) is rip and ship with auction pricing. It is still one buyer per sealed unit, so it is not a group break.
- A "mystery pack" or "repack" is NOT a manufacturer-sealed product. The seller makes it. That is the main legal difference from rip and ship (California 319.3).
- Wheel spins, HP battles, pull games, and King of the Hill add a second random draw or a contest between buyers. Plain rip and ship has only the manufacturer's random pack-out. That is why platforms exempt it.
- RTYH and pull boxes are edge cases. One buyer pays, but the number of packs or the prize depends on chance beyond the pack itself. The game should treat them as separate from plain rip and ship.

### Gaps
- I found no single official glossary that defines "pack auction" as a term. The definition above is an inference.
- I did not read the full current text of Whatnot's Surprise Products policy page. The details come from a seller blog (2025) and a guide (2026).

## Economics: price per pack, fees, shipping, giveaways, revenue, sourcing

### Takeaway
On Whatnot, a US Pokemon seller pays about 8% commission (standard tier, falling to 6.5% at high volume) plus 2.9% + $0.30 processing on the full amount the buyer pays, shipping included. eBay Live uses 6% + 2.9% + $0.30. TikTok Shop is about 6% (an 8% rate is reported but unconfirmed). Low-price items lose a large share to the fixed $0.30 fee. I found no reliable source on the typical rip-and-ship markup over market pack price.

### Cited Findings
- Whatnot US Trading Card Games fees: 8% commission at the Standard tier (under $15,000 in sales per 4-week period). The rate drops in steps to 6.5% at $500,000 and up. Processing is 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction, on the total the buyer pays, including shipping and tax. — [Underpriced Whatnot guide 2026](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/selling-pokemon-cards-on-whatnot-guide-2026)
- Whatnot's fee page cites standard commission rates "as of September 2026" and custom rates "as low as 3%" for qualified high-volume sellers. Since 2026-09-21, commission depends on sales tier and category. — [Whatnot seller fees](https://www.whatnot.com/seller/fees); [EcomCalcTools](https://ecomcalctools.com/blog/whatnot-fees/)
- Whatnot net on the card price at the Standard tier: a $1 sale keeps $0.59 (41% in fees). $5 keeps $4.15. $25 keeps $21.97. $100 keeps $88.80. Ten $3 sales cost $6.30 in fees. One $30 sale costs $3.57. — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/selling-pokemon-cards-on-whatnot-guide-2026)
- Whatnot high-value promotion: 0% commission on the part of one item price above $1,500. Processing still applies to the full amount. Sports breaks and Surprise Products are excluded. — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/selling-pokemon-cards-on-whatnot-guide-2026); [EcomCalcTools](https://ecomcalctools.com/blog/whatnot-fees/)
- Whatnot has no listing fee. — [Whatnot seller fees](https://www.whatnot.com/seller/fees)
- eBay Live example fees: 6% commission + 2.9% + $0.30 processing, on the total sale including shipping, handling, and tax. No insertion fee. Live rates apply only to purchases made during the event. — [eBay Live seller pricing](https://www.ebay.com/ebaylive/seller-pricing)
- TikTok Shop: the public category referral-rate table (revised May 2026) lists 6% for most categories. Several seller reports say the rate rose to 8% on 2026-08-04. This is not confirmed in official documentation. — [beBOLD](https://www.bebolddigital.com/news/tiktok-shop-8-percent-referral-fee); [Crosslist](https://crosslist.com/blog/tiktok-shop-fees)
- Shipping (buyer usually pays): USPS Ground Advantage up to 4 oz quoted at $4.47. First-Class Mail letters for eligible cards at $0.78 to $1.36. Buyer-paid shipping adds 2.9% processing. Example: a $5 card with $4.47 shipping costs about $0.97 in fees, not $0.85. — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/selling-pokemon-cards-on-whatnot-guide-2026)
- Whatnot shipping rule: ship within two business days. A carrier drop-off scan counts as shipped. — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/selling-pokemon-cards-on-whatnot-guide-2026)
- "$1 start": one bid can sell the item for $1. Example floor: an $8 card, $0.50 supplies, and a $5 profit target need $13.50 back, so the floor at Standard fees is a $16 sale. — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/selling-pokemon-cards-on-whatnot-guide-2026)
- eBay Live sellers advertise "$1 Start on Sealed Pokemon Inventory and Pack Rips Live!" events. — [eBay Live event](https://www.ebay.com/ebaylive/events/E9GYHSzjkZTD6fBN)
- Giveaway rules on Whatnot: official rules, "NO PURCHASE NECESSARY" in capitals, "void where prohibited", eligibility, the prize and its approximate value, an odds statement, and a statement that Whatnot does not sponsor the giveaway. Bulk cards and cheap singles are common giveaway items. — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/selling-pokemon-cards-on-whatnot-guide-2026)
- Pokemon bulk sold on eBay for a median of about 5 cents per card (172 lots, 2026-07-21 to 2026-10-03). — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/selling-pokemon-cards-on-whatnot-guide-2026)
- Sourcing: collections, local shops and card shows, sealed product from retail or from distributors (distributors usually want a business account), and the seller's own duplicates. — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/selling-pokemon-cards-on-whatnot-guide-2026)
- Example eBay Live sealed prices: a Mega Evolution Enhanced Booster Box listed at US $330 (21 sold); a 750-pack lot of 151 listed at US $15,000 (about $20 per pack). Single "on screen" rip-and-ship packs listed at about £8 to £27 (eBay.de). These are listings, not market averages. — [eBay listing search results](https://ebay.com/itm/405410835148); [eBay.de listing](https://www.ebay.de/itm/277409034223)
- Whatnot 2026 report: sellers who went live daily averaged about $60,000 or more per month (search summary of the Whatnot report; not confirmed on a primary page). — [search summary citing viewfromalove / Whatnot report](https://viewfromalove.com/blog/what-sells-best-on-whatnot/)
- Whatnot platform scale: over $8 billion gross merchandise sales in 2025, double 2024. Over 20 million accounts created in 2025. — [Benzinga (2026-03-18)](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/private-markets/26/03/51338094/whatnot-faces-rico-illegal-lottery-allegations-tied-to-sports-card-breaks)
- Whatnot says sellers who break are 4% of its sellers. — [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/private-markets/26/03/51338094/whatnot-faces-rico-illegal-lottery-allegations-tied-to-sports-card-breaks)

### Inferences
- Model for a game: net per pack = sale price x (1 - commission) - 2.9% x (sale price + shipping + tax) - $0.30 - cost of the pack - supplies - giveaway cost share. At a $5 pack price, the fixed $0.30 alone is 6% of the sale.
- Fees on a rip-and-ship order apply to the full price the buyer pays. There is no special fee rate for personals that I could find.
- A rip-and-ship seller makes margin from three sources: buying sealed product below market (distributor or case price), auction prices above market during hype, and volume. "$1 starts" put the risk on the seller. The seller accepts some sales below cost to keep bidding active.
- Buyers usually pay shipping, but one shipping charge per show often covers many items. So rip and ship of cheap packs becomes cheaper per pack when one buyer buys many packs in one show.

### Gaps
- No reliable source gave the typical rip-and-ship price per pack versus market price (for example, "packs sell at 110% of TCGplayer market"). Searches returned only single listings.
- No source gave typical revenue per stream, viewer count per stream, or hours per stream for Pokemon rip and ship.
- No source gave distributor or case cost as a percentage of MSRP for 2026.
- No source described a formal "buyer appreciation" program. The term is common in streams but I found no citable description.
- The exact TikTok Shop fee for trading cards is unconfirmed (6% official table vs. 8% seller reports).

## Audience dynamics

### Takeaway
Pokemon is very large on Whatnot: more than 10 million Pokemon cards sold in September 2026, and buyer count more than doubled in one year. The largest Pokemon seller audiences have about 640,000 followers. I found little citable data on what drives viewers. Viral clips (good or bad) clearly spread fast on X.

### Cited Findings
- Whatnot 2027 Consumer Trends Report (2026-10-20): four Pokemon cards sell every second on average. More than 10 million Pokemon cards sold on Whatnot in September 2026. Pokemon card order volume and buyer count each more than doubled over the past year, partly because of the 30th anniversary. Top searches: Pikachu, Charizard, Eevee, Gengar, Mewtwo. — [The Toy Book](https://toybook.com/whatnot-2027-consumer-trends-report-news/)
- Whatnot leaderboard (data as of 2026-10-12): vortexbreaks is #1 in Trading Card Games with 639.2K followers and 1.8M items sold; its shows are almost all Pokemon (sealed booster boxes, new sets, graded slabs, vintage). novatcg has 634.6K followers. — [viewfromalove](https://viewfromalove.com/blog/most-followed-whatnot-sellers/)
- A third-party scrape of the top 50 Whatnot Pokemon sellers found that 48 sellers in the top 50 on both days sold 41,380 items in one day. outsidehits was among the fastest movers. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/cleanscrape/who-sold-the-most-pokemon-cards-on-whatnot-in-a-day-two-checks-of-the-top-50-sellers-2dh6) (search summary; not read in full)
- Clip virality example: in February 2025, a clip of a Whatnot streamer who destroyed a "demi-god" pack spread on X. The viewer had won the pack in a giveaway through the free entry form, not a purchase. Fans criticized the streamer across social media. — [Dexerto (2025-02-19)](https://www.dexerto.com/pokemon/pokemon-tcg-streamer-under-fire-for-destroying-god-pack-viewer-won-fair-and-square-3137618/)
- Exposure example: viewers caught a father and son on a Whatnot stream (host "Pokerez609") when one pack showed two identical Pokeball reverse holos. A reupload of the stream spread. — [Dexerto (2025-09)](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/dad-caught-teaching-son-to-scam-pokemon-fans-with-resealed-packs-3246738/)
- Seller advice: go live on a regular schedule, answer condition questions on camera, ship what you showed. — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/selling-pokemon-cards-on-whatnot-guide-2026)

### Inferences
- Viewers watch for the chance of a big pull, and a big pull on camera is shareable content. The same camera that makes a hype clip also makes a scandal clip. A game can model "clip risk" both ways: a big hit raises followers; a caught trick destroys reputation.
- Giveaways grow the audience but carry a legal rule ("no purchase necessary"). A seller who treats free entrants badly gets public backlash.

### Gaps
- No citable data on follower growth rates, conversion from viewer to buyer, or the effect of giveaways on sales.
- No citable source on "chase boards" as a format on Pokemon streams.

## Controversies and risks

### Takeaway
The main risks are: resealed or pre-searched packs (alleged in specific cases), shill bidding (Whatnot reports it fell about 80% after enforcement in 2026), unshipped orders (Blake Martinez ban, 2023), and gambling-law claims against breaks and repacks (pending, no ruling). Plain rip and ship of manufacturer-sealed product is the lowest-risk format legally, but the pack-tampering risk stays.

### Cited Findings
- Resealed packs (2025-09): viewers accused a father and son on Whatnot (host "Pokerez609") of selling resealed packs. A pack showed two identical Pokeball reverse holos, one behind an Energy card. Dexerto reports the only real hits were one EX and those two reverse holos. Reports describe this as alleged scamming. I found no report of a ban or criminal charge. — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/dad-caught-teaching-son-to-scam-pokemon-fans-with-resealed-packs-3246738/); [WeGotThisCovered](https://wegotthiscovered.com/social-media/father-assures-son-theyll-keep-scamming-pokemon-fans-after-viewers-catch-them-red-handed-on-stream/)
- Resealing method: open packs, remove valuable cards, reseal, and sell as new. — [WeGotThisCovered](https://wegotthiscovered.com/social-media/father-assures-son-theyll-keep-scamming-pokemon-fans-after-viewers-catch-them-red-handed-on-stream/)
- Pack weighing (allegation): one eBay buyer complained that a seller's packs were pre-weighed "light packs" and that 15 packs gave no hits. This is a single unverified complaint. — [eBay listing feedback via search](https://ebay.com/itm/405328179645)
- Shill bidding: Whatnot reported (2026-07-28) that shill bidding activity "declined nearly 80%" over six months, user reports fell 45%, and its detection signals increased fivefold. Whatnot gave no totals. Penalties: warnings, restrictions, suspensions, or permanent bans. Detection looks at account relationships, device data, bidding patterns, and suspicious cancellations. The Trust and Safety team more than doubled in size and is the largest function in the company. — [Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/whatnot-shill-bidding-report/)
- Definition: shill bidding is when a seller, or someone connected to the seller, places bids with no real intent to buy, to raise the price. — [Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/whatnot-shill-bidding-report/)
- Critics: a legal filing reportedly says Whatnot has "historically prioritized platform growth over protecting the hobby" and often failed to give permanent bans. — [Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/whatnot-lawsuit-could-mean-big-trouble-for-breakers-help-protect-collectors)
- Whatnot breaks policy bans participation by the seller, employees, household or family members, or anyone with shared accounts. — [Whatnot Card Breaks Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107485220237-Card-Breaks-Policy)
- Unshipped orders: Blake Martinez (former NFL linebacker, "Blakes Breaks") — see the creators section.
- Gambling law, arbitration (2026-03): attorney Paul Lesko filed 15 arbitration demands for 30 clients against Whatnot. The claims say randomized box breaks and repack breaks are illegal lotteries under California law and violate RICO. Relief sought: a finding of illegal gambling, damages, warnings, spending limits, self-exclusion tools, and an end to randomized breaks. — [Benzinga (2026-03-18)](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/private-markets/26/03/51338094/whatnot-faces-rico-illegal-lottery-allegations-tied-to-sports-card-breaks)
- Later reports say nearly 70 Whatnot customers filed arbitration demands. — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/insights/102o3q3/the-future-of-card-breaking-and-repacks-insights-from-california-litigation) (conflict with the 30-client figure in March; the number likely grew)
- Lesko said he made the arbitration public partly because Whatnot changed its arbitration terms in March 2026 to split fees with consumers. Users have a 30-day window to opt out of arbitration. — [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/private-markets/26/03/51338094/whatnot-faces-rico-illegal-lottery-allegations-tied-to-sports-card-breaks)
- Qui tam suit (filed 2025-07, unsealed later): under the California False Claims Act, it names Whatnot and Fanatics Live. It alleges unlicensed box-break lotteries and unpaid gambling taxes and license fees. The California Department of Justice declined to intervene. The plaintiffs may proceed. — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/insights/102o3q3/the-future-of-card-breaking-and-repacks-insights-from-california-litigation)
- Status: no arbitrator or court has ruled that randomized breaks or repacks are illegal gambling. Whatnot denies the claims: "We absolutely reject the characterization in this complaint" and "Gambling isn't allowed on Whatnot, and we strictly enforce this policy." — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/insights/102o3q3/the-future-of-card-breaking-and-repacks-insights-from-california-litigation); [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/private-markets/26/03/51338094/whatnot-faces-rico-illegal-lottery-allegations-tied-to-sports-card-breaks)
- Lottery test (California Penal Code 319): consideration (the buyer pays), chance (a random tool assigns the team or card), and a prize. — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/insights/102o3q3/the-future-of-card-breaking-and-repacks-insights-from-california-litigation)
- Minors: Whatnot permits users aged 13 and older; ages 13 to 17 need a parent's permission. — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/selling-pokemon-cards-on-whatnot-guide-2026)
- Lesko argues Whatnot is addictive and lacks self-ban and spending-limit tools that online casinos must provide. — [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/private-markets/26/03/51338094/whatnot-faces-rico-illegal-lottery-allegations-tied-to-sports-card-breaks)
- Presales risk on eBay: a buyer can find out the seller will not deliver only after the normal claim period ends. — [Value Added Resource on eBay presales](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-pokemon-presales-fraud-risks/)
- One 2026 analysis calls rip and ship a visible fraud risk that the platform has limited incentive to police (opinion). — [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/61932be87f70a3d6)

### Inferences
- In plain rip and ship, the buyer owns a manufacturer-sealed product before the opening. The buyer pays for a known item, not for a random assignment. That makes the "chance" element weaker than in a random team break. No court has ruled on this, so this is an inference.
- The pack-tampering risk is the main fraud risk unique to rip and ship: resealing, pre-searching, weighing, and swapping a hit off camera. The Whatnot rule that the product must stay on screen is the platform's main control against swaps.
- Chargebacks: I found no citable source specific to rip and ship. A game can still model them as a normal marketplace risk.

### Gaps
- No citable source on "fake pulls" (a seller who plants a hit) in a named Pokemon case.
- No citable data on chargeback rates for live card sales.
- No court ruling exists as of November 2026. The arbitration outcomes are unknown.
- I found no state-by-state survey for the US. Only California law is covered in the sources.

## Rules: platform rules, TPCi stance, state law

### Takeaway
Whatnot exempts rip and ship from most break rules but requires full on-camera visibility. Whatnot bans wheels, machines, prizes in breaks, and paid bounties. TikTok Shop US (2026-06-25) banned eight break formats and all spins, but still permits personal breaks for sellers with a Shop Performance Score of 2.5 or higher. eBay limits breaks and repacks to pre-approved sellers (repacks from 2026-06-15). I found no public TPCi statement on rip and ship.

### Cited Findings
- Whatnot breaks: no prizes (including "Prize Picks"); no paid bounties, guarantees, or bonus items tied to outcomes; no breaks that span shows; no off-platform presales of spots; no non-card items; rules (what counts as "base" or "bulk") must appear in show notes. Unfilled breaks: honor refund requests; the seller may keep selling spots in a later show if the product is still sealed. — [Whatnot Card Breaks Policy (2026-06-22)](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107485220237-Card-Breaks-Policy)
- Whatnot Gambling and Purchase-Based Prize Policy prohibits incentives for high bids and prizes based on the outcome of a purchase-based game. — [Whatnot Gambling and Purchase-Based Prize Policy (search summary)](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/4410443596813)
- Whatnot formats that try to get around the Surprise Sets policy get discovery limits and enforcement. — [Whatnot Card Breaks Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107485220237-Card-Breaks-Policy)
- Whatnot Coins and Money Surprise policy limits surprise products in that category to collectible coins and bullion from 2026-01-13 (shows that Whatnot tightens surprise rules category by category). — [Whatnot help center (search summary)](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/40686320982029-Coins-Money-Surprise-Policy)
- TikTok Shop US gambling policy (2026-06-25): bans Random Team, Draft-Style, Bounty, Pull Games, Points Program, Train Games, King of the Hill, and HP Battles. Permits Personal Breaks, Pick Your Team or Type, Pull-Til-You-Win, and Case Breaks. Sellers need a Shop Performance Score of 2.5 or higher and must complete the Qualification Center process. Random team assignment is allowed only with a manufacturer-sealed surprise set. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shop-kills-8-break-formats-and-bans-lucky-spins-under-gambling-rules/)
- TikTok Shop UK help pages say break livestreams are allowed there; one source says the ban is US only. — [TikTok Shop UK trading cards](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=3291731840829206) (search summary)
- eBay Live: breaks only by pre-approved, vetted sellers. — [eBay breaks page](https://pages.ebay.com/breaks)
- eBay repacks: announced in early May 2026, effective 2026-06-15. "only pre-approved sellers will be permitted to list and sell repacks on eBay." The application asks about odds and contents disclosure and standard checklists. eBay cited "greater transparency, fairness, and trust". — [Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-trading-card-repack-rules-live/)
- California law: Penal Code 319 (general lottery: consideration, chance, prize) and 319.3 (sports trading card grab bags = repacks). The 319.3 definition does not appear to reach a manufacturer's original sealed pack. Other states use different gambling definitions. — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/insights/102o3q3/the-future-of-card-breaking-and-repacks-insights-from-california-litigation)
- ESPN published an article on whether box breaking could be gambling or a lottery (date not verified). — [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/49098946/could-box-breaking-considered-gambling-lottery) (not read in full)

### Inferences
- For the game, a legal rip-and-ship stream needs: product on camera from sale to reveal, no wheel or spin, no prize tied to an outcome, no seller or family bidding, shipping within two business days, and giveaways with free entry.
- The trend from 2025 to 2026 is clear: every major platform tightened rules on random formats (Whatnot surprise products 2025-03 and 2025-11; TikTok 2026-06; eBay 2026-06). Plain rip and ship survived each change.
