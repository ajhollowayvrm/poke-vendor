# Counterfeit Risk

Fake cards and resealed boxes are a real part of the hobby this game
mirrors, so they get a real mechanic — not everywhere, only where real
counterfeiting actually happens: old sealed product, and singles bought
from a source that isn't official retail.

## Where the risk lives

Risk depends on the *product's age and channel of origin*, not the
storefront label alone.

### Sealed product

- **Pokemon Center, Amazon (new), camping a physical drop, local
  stores and the local game shop** (see
  [12-acquiring-product.md](12-acquiring-product.md)): none. Always
  current-release stock, straight from an official supply chain.
- **Hyped Reseller, garage/estate sales**: the only sources of
  out-of-print sealed product, and out-of-print sealed product always
  carries some reseal/counterfeit risk in real life — a box that is
  decades old, regardless of how trustworthy the seller seems. Risk
  scales with how old and how hyped the product is: an old, cheap,
  unremarkable box is rarely worth faking; a vintage chase box is.
- **eBay, Facebook Marketplace** (see
  [12-acquiring-product.md](12-acquiring-product.md)): the highest risk
  of all the channels. Individual sellers list their own product, so a
  fake or a resealed product can appear on any item, new or old. The
  same channels also have the best deals. The relative risk between
  the two is a balancing task.
- **Distributor/wholesale, case splits** (reputation-gated, see
  [04-reputation-and-followers-unlocks.md](04-reputation-and-followers-unlocks.md#reputation-tiers)):
  none. Part of what reputation is buying is trust in the supply chain
  itself.

### Singles

- **Garage/estate sales** (the Sourcer's channel, see
  [02-playstyles.md](02-playstyles.md)): the primary source of fake
  vintage singles — an unsorted lot from someone who does not know card
  values is exactly where an old fake surfaces unnoticed.
- **Meets and shows, Hyped Reseller, the local game shop's display
  case**: risk depends on the counterparty,
  not the venue. A high-reputation trading circle (see
  [04-reputation-and-followers-unlocks.md](04-reputation-and-followers-unlocks.md))
  is low risk; an anonymous public-table stranger or a bottom-tier
  online reseller is not.

## Risk is hidden at purchase

No storefront shows counterfeit risk. The player sees a price and the
stock, never a risk label. The Hyped Reseller does not care if an item
turns out fake, because it moves so much product. The eBay seller
rating is the one signal a player sees. It is a real eBay feature, not
a risk label, and a player can use it as a warning. The player finds a
fake only through the detection checks below.

## Detection: the same reveal pattern as grading

Counterfeit detection reuses the eyeball/paid/permanent-upgrade shape
already established in [10-grading.md](10-grading.md), rather than
inventing a new one:

- **Free eyeball check**: obvious tells — wrong cardstock thickness,
  off color saturation, printing errors, an incorrect card back.
  Reliability depends on the fake's quality tier (below).
- **Paid authentication**: a dedicated single-card authentication
  check, cheaper and faster than full grading, for a precise real/fake
  read on one card.
- **Permanent upgrade** (see [09-upgrades.md](09-upgrades.md)): an
  authentication tool that reveals real/fake for free, on every card,
  forever — the same durable-investment pattern as the grading
  subgrade-reveal upgrades.
- **Grading submission itself is a backstop**, even with no upgrade
  bought: PSA/BGS/CGC reject and flag a counterfeit during submission
  instead of returning a numeric grade. This still costs the
  submission fee and ties up the card for the full turnaround time, and
  it is not free of consequence if the player already resold or hyped
  the card as real before it comes back (see "Consequences" below).

A grading company's real/fake call is treated as final. There is no
laundering a flagged counterfeit into a real slab by resubmitting or
switching companies, mirroring how this actually works in the hobby.

## Fake quality tiers

Not one fake fidelity level — mirrors the grader-variance design in
[10-grading.md](10-grading.md#grader-variance):

- **Obvious bootleg**: wrong font, wrong color, cheap stock. The free
  eyeball check almost always catches it.
- **Convincing fake**: correct-feeling stock and printing, passes a
  casual eyeball, needs the paid check or the upgrade tool to catch.
- **Professional counterfeit**: a near-perfect reproduction. Even the
  upgrade tool carries a small residual miss chance — only a grading
  company's full authentication is fully reliable against this tier.

## Consequences

- **Bought a fake, caught it yourself (or via grading rejection)**: the
  cash spent does not come back — a straightforward loss, consistent
  with the game's existing risk/bust design (see
  [01-premise-and-loop.md](01-premise-and-loop.md#variance-and-busts)).
- **Unknowingly resold a fake, and the buyer finds out**: this is a
  scam accusation, handled by the existing mechanic in
  [06-social-media.md](06-social-media.md#consequences) — reputation
  and/or follower backlash applies even though the seller did not know,
  mirroring how the hobby's community reacts on intent-blind terms.

## Open topics

- Exact per-channel and per-quality-tier probability values — a
  balancing task, not a design gap, same as the reputation-point
  values in [04-reputation-and-followers-unlocks.md](04-reputation-and-followers-unlocks.md#status).
- Whether a player can *knowingly* pass on a fake as a deliberate
  choice, rather than only ever discovering fakes after the fact.
- The risk values for singles on eBay and Facebook Marketplace, if
  those channels sell singles (see
  [12-acquiring-product.md](12-acquiring-product.md#open-topics)).
- Interaction with a grading appeal/resubmission mechanic, if one gets
  designed (see [10-grading.md](10-grading.md#open-topics)).
