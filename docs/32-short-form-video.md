# Short-form video

**Status: built** (`Model/Shorts.swift`, `Social/ShortsViews.swift`). The numbers are first guesses. See
[Open topics](#open-topics).

Short-form video is a new content type in the one social media presence
(see [06-social-media.md](06-social-media.md)). The player makes a
short from something that happened in the game: a rip, a day of the
business, a show visit, or a grading return. Most shorts belong to a
**series**. A series builds momentum with each episode and ends with a
finale.

The game keeps one social presence. It does not split into TikTok and
YouTube. The rules below take the parts that both platforms share.

## Research (2026)

Creators post these card formats as short-form video:

| Format | What happens | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rip until I hit | The creator rips until a pull reaches a target value or a chase card. | The open end and the near misses keep viewers watching. |
| Rip and give away | The same, but the hit goes to a viewer or a kid. | Good will and new followers. |
| P&L challenge | "Day 14: I started with $100." Each short shows the running profit or loss. | Viewers come back each day for the result. |
| Daily series | "One pack a day for 365 days." | A fixed schedule brings viewers back. |
| Show vlog | Buys, haggles, and trades at a card show. | The deal is the story. |
| Grading reveal | "PSA 10 or not?" for a batch of slabs. | Suspense in a few seconds. One reveal of 16 cards had about 34,000 likes. |

- Plain pack openings are now too common. Openings with a hook or a
  challenge do better.
- Some viewers see rip content as gambling. One large streamer destroyed
  cards on stream to mock the trend.

What short-form video pays:

| Source | Real numbers |
| --- | --- |
| YouTube Shorts ad share | About $0.03 to $0.10 for each 1,000 views. |
| TikTok Creator Rewards | About $0.40 to $1.00 for each 1,000 qualified views. Only videos of more than 1 minute qualify. The program needs 10,000 followers. |
| Brand deals | The largest source. About $500 to $2,000 for each post at 100,000 followers. |
| Shop affiliate links | About 13% commission. |

**The lesson for the game:** views pay very little. The money comes from
what the followers bring: sponsor deals, stream viewers, and sales.

Sources: Primagames (a rip that ran long), Lemon8 ("can't eat until I
make money opening packs"), chaindesk ("Complete set in 151 minutes"),
Pokemon Pricing (openings are too common), the TikTok Pokémon TCG
discover page (grading reveals), Ginx (the gambling backlash),
InfluencerMarketingHub and AIR Media-Tech (Shorts RPM),
InfluencerMarketingHub and ttcalculator (TikTok Creator Rewards), Stack
Influence (brand deals), Branvas (affiliate commission).

## What a short is

- **A short needs a social account**, as every post does.
- **A short takes editing time:** 20 minutes for a normal episode, and
  45 minutes for a long video (a finale or a show vlog). The time comes
  out of the day. A short cannot start after 11 PM.
- **A short reaches people through the algorithm, not only through
  followers.** A small account can get many views, and a short goes
  viral more often than a quick post. See [Reach](#reach).
- **A short is a post** for the other social rules. It counts toward the
  3 posts of a day that gain followers.
- **A short adds 0.05 burnout**, less than a quick post (0.12 to 0.2). A
  series is planned content, not spam. Two daily series add 0.1 a day,
  the same as the daily recovery. Quick posts on top still raise it.
- **A short shows true numbers.** The game makes the short from what
  happened. The player cannot change the value of a pull or the P&L.

## Series

A series is a run of episodes in one format.

- **Start.** The social hub has "Start a series". The player picks a
  format and its settings. The game names the series, for example
  "Rip until I hit $500".
- **Limits.** The player can run 2 series at a time, and one series of
  each format.
- **One episode a day** for each series.
- **Momentum** starts at 1.0. Each episode adds 0.1, up to 2.0. Momentum
  multiplies the reach of the episodes.
- **Missed days.** The daily formats (Rip until I hit, and the P&L
  challenge) expect an episode each day. Each day with no episode takes
  0.15 off the momentum, down to 1.0. After 3 days in a row with no
  episode, the series dies: followers −1% and authenticity −0.05. The
  event formats (show vlogs and grading reveals) lose 0.1 of momentum
  each week with no episode, and they do not die.
- **The finale** is a long video. It has 2 times the reach, and it can
  gain 2 times the normal follower limit.
- **Ending early.** "End series" stops a series. A daily series that
  ends before its finale costs authenticity −0.05 ("the creator quit").

## Formats

### Rip until I hit

The player rips, on film, until a card reaches the target.

- **Settings:** a target value: $100, $250, $500, $1,000, or $5,000.
- **Filming.** While the series runs, a rip in Normal or Fast mode can be
  an episode. Sift and Hits only cannot, because the viewers want to see
  each reveal (as on a live stream). The summary of the last pack has
  "Post episode", or "Post the finale".
- **An episode** is one rip, the whole queue. The series counts the packs, the
  money spent (the amount paid for the opened product), and the best
  card so far.
- **Episode quality** comes from the best card of the rip, as a pull
  reveal does. A **near miss** (a card of 70% of the target or more)
  adds 0.3. Each 25 packs in the series adds 0.1, up to 0.5, because the
  suspense grows.
- **The finale** is the rip that pulls a card at or above the target. The
  rip summary shows "Post the finale". The finale quality adds 0.5, and
  more for a hit after a long series.
- **Give it away.** The finale can give the hit away to a viewer. The
  card leaves Inventory. Followers gain 3 times the finale limit,
  authenticity +0.10, and reputation +2. A giveaway always costs the full
  value of the card.
- **The gambling backlash.** After $1,000 spent in a series with no hit,
  each episode has a 20% chance of a backlash: comments say "this is
  just gambling". It costs authenticity −0.03.

### Record & Rip

The player films a rip on purpose for the running series.

- **The button.** A sealed item has "Record & Rip" when a running series
  can take a rip of it. The button is in Inventory select mode and on
  the item's detail screen. It is not there when no series can take the
  rip.
- **Which series.** "Rip until I hit" takes any rip. The P&L challenge
  takes a rip of items that are in the challenge, or that can all join
  it (the same rules as "Challenge", and the challenge cash must cover
  all of them). A series with an episode today takes no more.
- **The rip records for every series that can take it.** Items that can
  join the P&L challenge join it before the rip, so the pulls and the
  bulk join too.
- **A recorded rip** shows "REC" next to the mode control. It allows
  Normal and Fast only. Sift and Hits only are off, because the viewers
  want to see each reveal.
- **The summary of the last pack** says which series the rip was
  recorded for. It has a post button for each one: "Post episode" (or
  the finale) for Rip until I hit, and "Post to the challenge" (or the
  P&L finale) for the P&L challenge. Each post takes its own editing
  time.
- **A P&L episode from a rip** shows the day, the value, the P&L, and the
  best pull. Its quality is the better of the P&L quality and the
  quality of the best pull.

### P&L challenge

The player runs a small business inside the business, and posts the
result each day.

- **Settings:** a start amount ($50, $100, $500, or $1,000), a goal (2,
  5, or 10 times the start), and a day limit (14, 30, or 60 days).
- **The challenge cash.** The start amount is a book number, not a
  second wallet. The cash stays in the one wallet.
- **Challenge items.** The player adds items to the challenge from
  Inventory: select mode has "Challenge". Each item takes its amount
  paid out of the challenge cash. The item must have no status, and it
  must be bought on or after the day the challenge started. A pulled card
  cannot be added on its own.
  - The cards from a rip of a challenge product join the challenge. So
    does the bulk group of that rip.
  - A sale of a challenge item, on any channel, puts the money after
    fees and shipping into the challenge cash. A lot puts in the share
    of the challenge items, by market.
  - A challenge item does not trade: not to a vendor, a trader, or a
    seller. The challenge counts cash.
  - An item in a lot or in a vending machine is out of the value until
    it sells.
  - A challenge item has a "CHALLENGE" tag.
- **The challenge value** is the challenge cash plus the market value of
  the challenge items. The P&L is the value minus the start amount.
- **An episode** shows the day, the value, and the P&L, for example
  "Day 9: $100 → $164 (+64%)". Episode quality comes from the change
  since the last episode, up or down. A big swing gets more views than a
  quiet day, so a loss is content too.
- **The finale** comes when the value reaches the goal (a win), or on the
  last day (the result). A win adds 1.0 to the quality. A loss at the end
  has normal quality, and no penalty. Ending early costs authenticity.
- When the challenge ends, its items go back to normal Inventory.

### Show vlogs

- **After a show or a meet,** the summary has "Post a show vlog". A vlog
  is a long video, and it is an episode of the "Card show vlogs" series.
  The game starts that series the first time.
- **Vlog quality** comes from the visit: the number of deals, the best
  deal (the most market value over the price), the counters that the
  player won, the trades, the stash buys, a showcase sale, and the
  showcase on the table. A visit with no deal makes a weak vlog.

### Grading reveals

- **When a grading submission comes back,** the social hub's "New short"
  lists it for 7 days. "Post the reveal" makes a short of the
  submission. It is an episode of the "Grading reveals" series.
- **Reveal quality** comes from the number of cards, the 10s, a Black
  Label or a Pristine 10, and the value change from raw to slab. A
  submission of 10 or more cards adds 0.2.

## Reach

```
views = (200 + followers × 0.25) × quality × momentum × timing × luck
        × (1 − burnout × 0.7) × (0.4 + authenticity) × production
```

- The base is higher than a quick post (40 + followers × 0.35) for a
  small account, and lower for a large one. Short-form reach comes from
  the algorithm more than from the follower count.
- **Luck** is the same roll as a post, but a short goes viral 4% of the
  time (a post: 2%), at 8 to 30 times the reach.
- **A finale** has 2 times the reach.
- **Followers gained** follow the post rule: views × the gain rate, up to
  the daily limit. A finale has 2 times the limit.

## Pay

| Source | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| View pay | From 10,000 followers (tier 2). A normal episode (under 1 minute) pays $0.06 for each 1,000 views. A long video (a finale or a vlog) pays $0.60 for each 1,000 views. The pay collects, and it arrives every 7 days as "Creator payouts" in the ledger. |
| Sponsors | While a series runs, a sponsor offer comes 1.5 times as often. A paid post can be a series episode. A sponsored episode costs authenticity −0.02, not −0.04, because the read fits the video. |
| Stream traffic | A short with 5,000 views or more adds 10% to the viewers of the player's next stream in 3 days. |

The view pay is small on purpose. A viral finale of 2,000,000 views pays
about $1,200. A normal episode of 20,000 views pays about $1.20.

## Screens

- **The social hub** gets a Series section. Each running series shows
  its format, its episode count, a momentum meter, and its progress: the
  packs and the best card against the target, or the P&L against the
  goal. It has "Post today's episode" when an episode is ready, and "End
  series".
- **"Start a series"** opens a sheet with the formats and their settings.
- **The Shorts box** of the social hub lists the running series, the
  vlog of today's visit, and the grading reveals of the last 7 days, each
  with "Post". With an accepted sponsor deal, "Sponsored episode" makes
  the next short a paid post.
- **The rip summary** of the last pack has "Post episode" or "Post the
  finale", and on a finale "Post the finale and give the card away".
- **The show summary** has "Post a show vlog".
- **Inventory select mode** has "Challenge" while a P&L challenge runs.
- **Inventory select mode and the sealed detail screen** have "Record &
  Rip" when a running series can take the rip.
- **The analytics upgrade** shows the reach factors of each short,
  momentum included.

## Out of scope

- **Separate platforms.** One social presence stays (see
  [06-social-media.md](06-social-media.md)).
- **Trade-up challenges.** The stashes and trades can carry this format
  later.
- **Pack battles** against another creator.
- **Group breaks.** The docs exclude them (see
  [12-acquiring-product.md](12-acquiring-product.md)).

## Open topics

- The quality numbers are first guesses. A playtest must check that a
  normal player gains followers from shorts at a rate close to quick
  posts, and that a viral finale is a rare event.
- The P&L challenge needs a check of each sale path, so that every sale
  of a challenge item reaches the challenge cash.
