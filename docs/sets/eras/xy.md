# XY era (2014–2016)

This file records the pack template that most English XY-era sets
share. Each set file records its own data and its exceptions. See
[../../13-sets.md](../../13-sets.md) for what a set file covers, and
[../../18-ripping.md](../../18-ripping.md#where-the-hit-sits) for why
pack order matters.

### Excluded products (no booster packs of their own)

- **Kalos Starter Set** (KSS, November 8, 2013, 39 cards). It was
  three theme decks. The standard decks had no booster pack. The
  deluxe decks had one Black & White Series pack, not a Kalos Starter
  Set pack.
- **XY Trainer Kit**. Bulbapedia lists no booster packs in it.
- **McDonald's Collection 2014, 2015, and 2016**. Each is a 12-card
  promotional set. Each toy came with one card. There were no
  booster packs.
- **Theme decks** of the era. They are not sets, and the decks checked
  contain no booster packs (see [Sealed products](#sealed-products)).

The research found no other English booster-pack set from this era.

## The pack template

### Structure

- **10 cards per pack, plus 1 code card.** Bulbapedia: "Since the
  release of the first Diamond & Pearl expansion, these consistently
  had 10 cards." Retail listings show "(10 Cards)". Confidence:
  **official product labeling**.
- **No separate basic Energy slot.** Bulbapedia states that the extra
  Energy card started with the Sun & Moon Series. When a set prints
  basic Energy cards, they come in the common slots. Confidence:
  **community wiki**.
- **Slot breakdown: 5 commons, 3 uncommons, 1 reverse holo, 1 rare
  slot.** Bulbapedia gives "3 Uncommon cards, and the remainder are
  Common", 1 guaranteed rare, and 1 reverse holo per pack. The
  ThePriceDex averages (5 commons, 3 uncommons) agree. Confidence:
  **community wiki and community estimate**.
- **Conflict:** one retailer (Pack Fresh, Primal Clash) lists
  "1 Rare, 3 Uncommons, 6 Commons, and 1 Reverse Holo". That makes 11
  cards, not 10. This file uses 5 commons.

### The rare slot

The rare slot always holds one card. The card is one of: a rare, a
holo rare, a Pokémon-EX, a full art (Ultra Rare), or a secret rare.
Pokémon BREAK cards (from BREAKthrough onward) sit in the reverse holo
slot, not the rare slot (see [Sets that break the
template](#sets-that-break-the-template)). In every template set,
ThePriceDex lists the non-holo rare at "1 in 1.5 packs". So about 1
pack in 3 has a holo rare or better in the rare slot.

### The reverse holo slot

Every pack has 1 reverse holo. Bulbapedia: a reverse holo version
"is usually available for every card in a set, excluding Ultra Rare
and Secret Rare cards, and any full-art cards". A reverse holo can be
a rare or a holo rare. Under the game's hit rule (rare or higher),
the reverse holo slot is sometimes a second hit.

### Pack order

The order below is the order that the player sees the cards, with the
card faces toward the player and the code card at the back.

| Position | Card | Confidence |
|----------|------|------------|
| 1–5 | Five cards (inferred: the commons) | Inferred |
| 6 | Reverse holo | Community guide |
| 7 | Rare slot (the hit) | Community guide |
| 8–10 | Three cards (inferred: the uncommons) | Inferred |
| 11 | Code card | Community guide |

**Evidence.** NinePocket gives this method for XY-era sets: "Pull the
code card off the back and set it aside. Move the back three cards to
the front of the stack. Flip through from the front — the rare lands
last, the reverse holo just before it." PokéPatch also gives "3 cards"
from the back to the front for the XY era. Reverse the method, and the
rare is card 7 of 10. The reverse holo is card 6.

**Inference.** NinePocket gives the Sun & Moon order from the back:
"code card, three uncommons, one basic Energy, then the rare, then a
reverse holo". The XY method moves one card fewer, and XY packs have
no Energy slot. This suggests that the three back cards in an XY pack
are the uncommons. No source states this for XY. Confidence:
**inferred**.

**Confidence of the whole order: community guide, era-wide.** No
source shows a real opened pack for a specific XY set. No source
describes each set separately.

**Exception to the game default.** The hit is **not** one of the last
three cards. The rare slot is card 7 of 10. Three cards follow it,
then the code card. The rip screen must put the rare slot at position
7 and the reverse holo at position 6 for template sets.

**A pack can open from either end.** The sources describe the stack
order, not the wrapper. No source says which wrapper face the code
card is behind. If a player takes the stack out reversed, the order
is reversed.

### The code card hint

The code card can show the rare slot tier before the player sees the
rare. Bulbapedia: a dark green back meant a regular rare. A "silver
and green background" meant "a Holofoil Rare, Ultra Rare or Secret
Rare". TheGamer says that in the TCG Online era, "a white code card
meant it was a good pack, while dark green was an average one".
Confidence: **community wiki and press article**. The sources do not
name the first and last sets with this hint.

### Rarity system

- Common, Uncommon, Rare (circle, diamond, star symbols).
- Rare Holo. Bulbapedia: "From XY to Steam Siege, the holofoil pattern
  is changed to 'Sheen Holofoil'."
- Rare Holo EX (Pokémon-EX and M Pokémon-EX).
- Rare Ultra (full art cards).
- Rare BREAK, from BREAKthrough.
- Rare Secret (numbered above the set total).
- Reverse holo pattern: "Emerging Powers to Steam Siege feature the
  card's respective Type symbol in the background of the card".

### Era-wide odds claims

Flipside Gaming gives era-wide figures. Its method is not stated.
Confidence: **community estimate**.

- "By Ancient Origins, pull rates had dropped to approximately 1:6
  packs for EXs and 1:12 packs for URs."
- BREAK: "around 1:12 booster packs, on par with URs, with some
  variance between sets."
- "Secret Rares continued to retain their 1:72 pack pull rate."

These figures conflict with the ThePriceDex per-set figures. Each set
file lists both.

### ThePriceDex data quality

ThePriceDex gives per-set odds. Its note says the rates are "based on
community data and may vary from actual pack openings". It does not
publish its method or sample sizes. Confidence: **community estimate
(aggregator)**. Three problems lower the confidence:

- Six sets share the same Ultra Rare ("1 in 17.0 packs") and secret
  rare ("1 in 125 packs") figures. Primal Clash and Roaring Skies
  share every rare-slot figure. These look like template values.
- For BREAKthrough, BREAKpoint, Fates Collide, and Steam Siege, the
  rare-slot figures add up to about 1.07 cards per pack. For
  Evolutions, they add up to about 1.18. One slot holds only 1 card.
  Either the figures are wrong, or BREAK or other cards sit outside
  the rare slot. No source resolves this.
- ThePriceDex assumes that "individual cards are assumed to have equal
  pull rates" within a rarity.

## Sealed products

- **Booster box: 36 packs.** ThePriceDex and retail listings give 36.
  Confidence: **retail listing and community estimate**.
- **Elite Trainer Box: 8 packs.** Bulbapedia lists 8 packs for every
  XY-series ETB except Generations (10 packs). Bulbapedia:
  "Plasma Freeze, Legendary Treasures, and Flashfire did not receive
  an Elite Trainer Box." Confidence: **community wiki**.
- **Theme decks: no booster packs.** Bulbapedia lists "a 60-card deck,
  damage counters, a custom coin, a rulesheet, a card list, a deckbox,
  a two-player mat, and a TCGO code card". This was checked for
  Brilliant Thunder (Flashfire) and Burning Spark (BREAKthrough).
  Bulbapedia also says XY theme decks have no packs.
- **Blisters:** every set had blisters. Pack counts are known only for
  XY (1, 2, and 3 packs).
- **Boxes per case: 6 boxes.** Potomac Distribution lists "Booster
  Case [6 boxes]" for XY, BREAKthrough, Phantom Forces, Steam Siege,
  and Ancient Origins. Blowout Cards lists "XY Evolution Booster 6 Box
  Case". This matches the 6-box case confirmed for the Sun & Moon,
  Sword & Shield, and Scarlet & Violet eras. Confidence: **retail
  listing**.
- **Collation:** Unknown. No source states a fixed number of hits per
  box. Model each pack as an independent draw until data says
  otherwise. This matches the approach in
  [../base-set.md](../base-set.md#box-and-case-structure).

## Sets that break the template

- **Double Crisis:** 7-card packs, sold only in 4-pack blisters. Every
  pack has 2 foil cards: a holo rare or EX, and a reverse holo. The
  pack order is Unknown.
- **Generations:** 10-card packs with 8 main-set cards and 2 Radiant
  Collection cards. No booster box. Sold only in collections and a
  10-pack ETB. The pack order is Unknown.
- **XY:** no secret rares.
- **Flashfire:** no ETB. The pack follows the template.
- **Phantom Forces:** one community wiki claims that holo Energy
  replaces the reverse holo in 1 in 6 packs. This is not verified.
- **BREAKthrough, BREAKpoint, Fates Collide, Steam Siege:** the BREAK
  rarity is added. **The BREAK card replaces the reverse holo, not the
  rare slot.** An Elite Fourum thread states: "They took the place of
  the reverse holo." Posters report "2 or 3 per booster box" (36
  packs), which matches the ThePriceDex BREAK rates of 1 in 12.9 to 1
  in 18. Confidence: **community forum report**. With BREAK removed
  from the rare-slot sum, the rare-slot figures for these four sets add
  up to about 1.0 card per pack, which resolves the earlier conflict.
- **Evolutions:** the secret rare odds are much higher in two sources
  than the era figure. The rare-slot odds do not add up to 1.

  support from a second guide on the 3-card method only.
- The identity of positions 1–5 and 8–10 is inferred, not sourced.
- Which wrapper face the code card sits behind is Unknown.
- The first and last sets with the code card rarity hint are Unknown.
- The ThePriceDex figures repeat across sets, and for five sets they
  add up to more than 1 card in the rare slot.
- Flipside era figures conflict with ThePriceDex per-set figures.
- Box collation is Unknown.
- Blister and collection pack counts are Unknown for every set except
  XY, Double Crisis, and Generations.

  XY-era set. The pack order stays an era-wide community claim.
