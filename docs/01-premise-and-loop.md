# Premise and Core Loop

## Starting point

You do not start with a storefront. A shop implies you already have stock,
staff, and customers — the game starts before that, as a solo trader with no
business, only yourself.

- You work from a bedroom, a car trunk, or a folding table at a swap meet.
- Your first goal is not sales. It is building capital and a name for yourself.
- You source cards from garage sales, estate sales, other traders, and packs
  you buy with your own money.
- You sell one-to-one: local groups, card show tables, meetups. No storefront,
  no staff, no overhead.
- Reputation starts as a personal thing — are you known as fair, or as a
  lowballer or a flake — not a business rating.
- Opening a real storefront, later, should be a decision that feels earned,
  not something that unlocks automatically at a number.

## What "making it" means

There is no fixed win screen. "Making it" means having enough of what you
personally value — cash, an audience, standing in the trading world, or a
collection you are proud of — to buy whatever you want at a show or store, or
just to feel like you have arrived. Play until it feels good.

See [03-currencies.md](03-currencies.md) for why this is tracked as four
separate numbers instead of one score.

## The four playstyles

Sourcing, flipping, content creation, and arbitrage are not separate classes.
They are tools in one toolbox. You are never locked into a path — every day
you choose which of them to use. See [02-playstyles.md](02-playstyles.md) for
detail on each.

## Day structure

A day is the atomic tick of the game. A single day can contain many actions.

### Free actions (no time cost, stack freely)

- Opening a pack you already own.
- Sending a card to a grader.
- Buying product online.
- Posting to social media (a quick post — see
  [06-social-media.md](06-social-media.md)).
- Listing a card online, responding to a trade offer.

### Time-cost actions (consume part or all of a day's budget)

- A local garage sale (partial day).
- A far garage sale (most of the day).
- Camping a store drop (large chunk of the day).
- A store run to local stores (partial day; each store adds time — see
  [12-acquiring-product.md](12-acquiring-product.md#local-stores-store-run-time-cost)).
- A Facebook Marketplace pickup (see
  [12-acquiring-product.md](12-acquiring-product.md)).
- Working a local meet (partial day).
- Going live on social media (time-cost, competes with the above).

### Multi-day commitments

- Card shows are multi-day blocks. Once committed, you are in for the whole
  block, and other single-day opportunities during that window pass you by.
- Mostly visible in advance, so the game rewards planning. Rare, disruptive
  surprise opportunities can appear with little or no lead time, forcing a
  fast decision to drop existing plans.

## Variance and busts

Time-cost actions carry real variance, including the chance of a bust — a
trip that costs time and returns nothing worthwhile. This is deliberate: if
every planned action pays off, planning stops mattering. The risk is the
point.
